# Ashes to Ashes: How the MMO Community's Last Great Hope Died in 52 Days

**The stunning collapse of Intrepid Studios and the death of the most anticipated MMO in a decade.**

---

## The Death of a Dream

On December 11th, 2025, the MMO community held its collective breath. After nearly a decade of promises, development updates, and over $3.25 million in crowdfunding, *Ashes of Creation* finally launched on Steam Early Access. For a genre that had watched World of Warcraft abandon its throne while nothing rose to claim it, this was supposed to be *the one*.

Fifty-two days later, it was dead.

On January 31st, 2026, founder and CEO Steven Sharif posted a cryptic message to Discord announcing his resignation "in protest." Within hours, WARN Act notices were being sent to over 200 developers. The studio couldn't make payroll. By February 2nd, Intrepid Studios would effectively cease to exist.

The question burning through gaming forums, Reddit threads, and Discord servers isn't just "what happened?" It's the darker question: *Was this the plan all along?*

---

## The Perfect Storm of Red Flags

To understand the collapse, you have to understand the man at the center of it.

Steven Sharif wasn't a game developer. He was a multi-level marketing executive—a top earner at XanGo, one of those "health supplement" companies that walks the razor's edge between aggressive sales tactics and regulatory action. When he announced he was "self-funding" an ambitious MMORPG in 2016, eyebrows raised. When the Kickstarter raised $3.25 million in under 12 hours using a referral program that *violated Kickstarter's own rules*, the eyebrows stayed raised.

But the gaming community wanted to believe. They *needed* to believe.

The MMO genre had become a graveyard of broken promises—*Wildstar*, *Warhammer Online*, *New World* stumbling around like a zombie that wouldn't stay dead. Here was a guy promising everything: a dynamic world where player actions literally reshape the map, 64 class combinations, meaningful PvP and PvE, player-driven economies and politics.

For years, Sharif positioned himself as the anti-corporate savior.

> "I am the sole share owner in the company. I don't have to answer to a board."

It was a powerful reassurance—*Ashes of Creation* would never be compromised by suits demanding monetization schemes or rushed releases.

Except it wasn't true.

---

## The Investors in the Shadows

Public filings and court documents paint a different picture entirely.

Months *before* the Kickstarter even launched, Intrepid Studios had entered into financing agreements with outside investors. The Ya-Ya Legacy Trust, operated by Jason Caramanis, pumped at least $2 million into the company, converting debt to equity and acquiring nearly 10% ownership. A June 2024 lawsuit revealed that Caramanis was denied access to financial records—a red flag that something was deeply wrong with the company's books.

More damning is the name now circulating through community discussions: **Karen Boreyko**.

Boreyko was a co-founder of Vemma, another MLM company that the FTC shut down as a pyramid scheme in 2015. People from that world often ended up at XanGo—the same MLM where Sharif made his fortune. According to community researchers digging through corporate filings, Boreyko appears to control Intrepid Studios' debts, suggesting her capital may have been the majority financing for the entire project.

The man who promised no corporate overlords had apparently been funded by MLM money from the start.

---

## The Steam Rush: Coincidence or Cash Grab?

The timing of the Steam Early Access launch now looks less like a milestone and more like a heist.

**December 11th, 2025.** *Ashes of Creation* hits Steam at $40 a copy. Despite being firmly in Alpha territory—missing features, bugs everywhere, core systems like the famous "Node" system barely functional—the game peaked at 31,800 concurrent players.

The numbers tell the story:

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| Copies Sold | 220,000 - 320,000 |
| Revenue (Pre-Platform Fees) | $11M - $16M |
| Days Until Shutdown | 52 |

Not a bad haul for a game that would be abandoned less than two months later.

The community had raised concerns for months. The game felt rushed to Steam. Features promised for years remained vaporware. Content creators who had covered the game religiously started sounding alarm bells. But Sharif was reassuring as ever—just "targeted adjustments," nothing to worry about.

On January 22nd, nine employees were laid off. "Targeted team adjustments," Sharif said. No effect on development.

Nine days later, he was gone. So was everyone else.

---

## "Actions I Could Not Ethically Support"

Sharif's resignation statement was carefully worded:

> "Control of the company shifted away from me, and the Board began directing actions that I could not ethically agree with or carry out. As a result, I chose to resign in protest rather than lend my name or authority to decisions I could not ethically support."

It's the statement of a man positioning himself as the victim of a hostile takeover. Much of the senior leadership followed him out the door, including Communications Director Margaret Krohn, who posted a heartfelt farewell acknowledging the situation "wasn't what I expected."

But the community isn't buying the martyrdom narrative.

> "Steven did a rug pull. According to the company's legal papers, he was the sole board member, so he was the only one in power."
> 
> — Steam Community Discussion

The contradiction is glaring. How does a man who claimed to be the sole shareholder lose control to a board? When did ownership actually transfer? What were these "unethical actions" he couldn't support—shutting down the project he'd just cashed out of?

The answers aren't forthcoming. Sharif cited "ongoing legal and governance matters" for why he couldn't explain further. Convenient.

---

## The Human Wreckage

Lost in the conspiracy theories and righteous anger is a genuine tragedy: **over 200 developers who just lost their jobs**.

These weren't the MLM executives or shadowy investors. These were game developers who spent years—in some cases, nearly a decade—trying to build something special. Many uprooted their lives, moved across the country, took below-market salaries because they believed in the vision.

Margaret Krohn addressed them directly in her farewell, urging the community to "be kind to developers who had just lost their jobs after years of work."

The WARN Act notices gave them until February 2nd. The studio reportedly couldn't meet its most recent payroll period. Workers who dedicated years of their careers to *Ashes of Creation* may not even get their final paychecks.

Sharif's statement acknowledged this:

> "The developers and staff acted in good faith and deserved better than the uncertainty they are now facing. I am incredibly dismayed by the situation."

*Dismayed.* An interesting word choice from a man who allegedly controlled the company that put them there.

---

## The Throne Stays Empty

And so the MMO genre returns to its wandering in the wilderness.

World of Warcraft Classic maintains its grip on nostalgia-driven players, but retail WoW continues its slow decline. *Final Fantasy XIV* remains solid but unchanged. *New World* just announced its own sunset for January 2027—apparently Amazon couldn't make an MMO work either. The genre that once defined online gaming continues to shrink, consolidate, and disappoint.

*Ashes of Creation* was supposed to be different. It had the funding. It had the ambition. It had a community so devoted they defended every delay, forgave every missed deadline, attacked every critic. They *wanted* this to work.

Instead, they got 52 days.

Some Kickstarter backers are pointing to pledge commitments promising refunds if the game failed to launch. Whether an "Early Access Alpha" counts as a launch—and whether there's any money left to refund—remains to be seen. Class action discussions are already brewing in Steam forums. The usual gaming lawyers are circling.

---

## The Verdict

Was *Ashes of Creation* a scam from day one, as the longtime skeptics claim? Or was it a legitimate passion project that collapsed under the weight of undisclosed debts and investor pressure, as Sharif's resignation suggests?

The truth is probably messier than either narrative.

What's undeniable is the pattern:

- ✓ A founder with MLM background
- ✓ Undisclosed investors with MLM connections
- ✓ Years of promises and delays
- ✓ A rushed Steam launch generating millions
- ✓ Immediate collapse leaving workers unpaid

Whether you call it a pump and dump, a hostile takeover, or simply catastrophic mismanagement, the result is the same.

**The most anticipated MMO of the decade is dead.** Hundreds of developers are unemployed. Millions of dollars from hopeful players have vanished into the void.

And the MMO community is left, once again, with nothing but ashes.

---

## What Happens Now?

The future of the *Ashes of Creation* IP remains unclear. Servers are still online as of this writing, though for how long is anyone's guess.

**For Kickstarter Backers:** Refund claims will likely need to be pursued through whatever liquidation process eventually emerges. The original pledge included language about refunds if the game failed to launch—whether Early Access counts as "launch" will be legally contested.

**For Steam Purchasers:** Standard refund requests are being denied under Steam's 2-hour/14-day policy. Some players are attempting legal arguments around "frustration of purpose" and "material breach of contract." Success rates appear low so far.

**For the Developers:** Many have already updated their LinkedIn profiles to "Open to Work." In a gaming industry still reeling from years of layoffs, finding new positions won't be easy.

Steven Sharif has not made any public statements beyond his resignation announcement.
